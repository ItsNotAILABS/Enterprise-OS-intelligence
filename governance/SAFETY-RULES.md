## Preamble

These Safety Rules and Safety Objectives establish the enterprise's binding commitments to safe operation of all intelligent systems, autonomous agents, and sovereign infrastructure. They are subordinate to the Master Charter, the Rules of Procedure, and the Code of Conduct.

Safety is not a feature to be toggled. Safety is a conservation law — like sovereignty, it must be continuously preserved or it is lost.

## Part I — Safety Rules

### Article 1 — Fundamental Safety Rules

#### SR-1: No Uncontrolled Autonomy

No autonomous agent or system may operate without:
- A defined scope boundary (chartered)
- A human override mechanism
- An observable state (operator dashboard)
- A kill switch (immediate deactivation path)

#### SR-2: No Irreversible Harm Without Human Authorization

No system may take an action that is:
- Irreversible, AND
- Potentially harmful to persons, data, or infrastructure

without explicit human authorization recorded in CHRONO.

#### SR-3: Evidence Before Action

No system may take consequential action based on unverified inputs. The truth-status ladder (Governing Principle P2) applies to all safety-critical decisions. Only inputs at `review_supported` or higher may trigger consequential actions.

#### SR-4: Fail-Safe Defaults

All systems must fail to a safe state. Specifically:
- Loss of connectivity → system preserves last known good state
- Ambiguous input → system requests clarification, does not act
- Agent conflict → escalate to human, do not resolve autonomously
- Data corruption detected → halt processing, alert operator

#### SR-5: Containment of Agent Scope

Each agent (ARCHON, VECTOR, LUMEN, FORGE) operates strictly within its chartered scope. Cross-scope actions require explicit coordination through the Agent Council with full audit trail.

#### SR-6: No Silent Failures

Every failure, error, or anomaly must produce an observable signal. Silent failure is a safety violation. The operator dashboard (Operator Charter) must surface all system health signals.

### Article 3 — Incident Response

#### 3.1 — Severity Classification

| Severity | Description | Response Time |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **CRITICAL** | Active sovereignty breach, data loss, uncontrolled agent action | Immediate (< 1 hour) |
| **HIGH** | Safety rule violation detected, system integrity at risk | < 4 hours |
| **MEDIUM** | Anomalous behavior, potential safety drift | < 24 hours |
| **LOW** | Minor non-compliance, no immediate risk | < 7 days |

#### 3.2 — Response Protocol

1. **Detect** — System monitoring or human observation identifies incident
2. **Contain** — Affected systems isolated or restricted immediately
3. **Assess** — Safety Board evaluates scope, cause, and impact
4. **Remediate** — Root cause addressed, safety constraints reinforced
5. **Record** — Full incident record committed to CHRONO (immutable)
6. **Review** — Post-incident review, lessons integrated into safety objectives

#### 3.3 — Escalation Authority

- CRITICAL incidents: Primary Builder notified immediately
- HIGH incidents: Safety Board convenes within response window
- MEDIUM/LOW: Committee of Standards handles through standard procedures

## Part II — Safety Objectives

### Article 4 — Strategic Safety Objectives

#### SO-1: Continuous Safety Verification

**Objective:** All safety-critical systems maintain real-time verifiable safety state.

**Measures:**
- Operator dashboard displays safety status for all organisms
- Safety constraints verified on every autonomous cycle
- Quarterly safety audits of all agent scopes and boundaries

#### SO-2: Sovereignty Preservation

**Objective:** No system interaction ever results in loss of sovereignty for any data subject or operator.

**Measures:**
- Sovereignty conservation verified at architecture level (Noether symmetry test)
- Sovereign Memory Vaults encrypted with vetKeys before data exists
- No sovereign data ever leaves the data subject's control without explicit consent

#### SO-3: Proportional Autonomy

**Objective:** Agent autonomy is proportional to the reversibility of their actions.

**Measures:**
- Low-reversibility actions require human authorization
- High-reversibility actions may proceed autonomously within scope
- Autonomy levels defined per agent per action class in agent charters

#### SO-4: Transparent Operation

**Objective:** Every system's operational state is observable by authorized operators at all times.

**Measures:**
- Six-section operator dashboard (Operator Charter)
- Real-time alert stream for anomalies
- Full CHRONO audit trail for all consequential actions

#### SO-5: Accumulative Safety Learning

**Objective:** The enterprise's safety posture improves with every incident, every cycle, and every deployment.

**Measures:**
- Incident records compound into precedent graph (REMEMBER substrate)
- Safety patterns identified by agent analysis feed back into safety rules
- φ-compounding memory ensures safety lessons are never lost

#### SO-6: Education System Safety

**Objective:** All education-facing systems (Bronze/Silver Canisters) maintain the highest safety standards for student interaction.

**Measures:**
- Student data classified SOVEREIGN — no external access
- Age-appropriate interaction boundaries enforced at the canister level
- Teacher dashboard provides full visibility into student-facing system behavior
- Behavioral laws L72–L79 (Bronze Canister Charter) enforced continuously

## Part III — Governance

### Article 6 — Safety Board

6.1. The Safety Board is the body responsible for:
- Maintaining and updating these Safety Rules
- Reviewing safety incidents and approving remediation
- Conducting quarterly safety audits
- Recommending safety-related architectural changes

6.2. Safety Board decisions on CRITICAL incidents take immediate effect (Rules of Procedure, Article 4.3).

6.3. Safety Board composition and procedures are defined in the Rules of Procedure (Article 2).

### Article 7 — Governance of These Rules

7.1. These Safety Rules are amended through the procedures defined in the Rules of Procedure (Article 6).

7.2. Safety Rules may only be amended to strengthen safety posture. No amendment may weaken an existing safety guarantee without Primary Builder explicit authorization and documented rationale.

7.3. These Rules do not expire.

7.4. These Rules take effect immediately upon publication.
